rag-eval-lab

Retrieval evaluation harness · open source

Everyone building with LLMs has a RAG demo; few can state their retrieval recall. This measures it: BM25, dense vectors and rank fusion across three chunking strategies, scored on recall@k, MRR and nDCG against human relevance judgments, with serving cost reported beside every quality number. The finding: finer chunks help dense retrieval and hurt BM25, and the two cross.

JobReady: privacy-preserving document understanding

Master's thesis
2025 to 2026 · supervised by Anis Ben Aicha · defence autumn 2026

A B2B staffing platform that turns unstructured CVs into queryable representations, matches them semantically to roles, and simulates interviews with language models. Three phases, three research problems: information extraction, representation learning and retrieval, and LLM-driven dialogue.

The design constraint: the language model never learns who the candidate is. Every CV is pseudonymised with Presidio and spaCy NER before any model call, then re-identified locally afterwards. That implements GDPR Article 5(1)(c) in the pipeline rather than in a policy document, and the EU AI Act classifies employment AI as high-risk.

Matching runs on text-embedding-3-small vectors in PostgreSQL with pgvector, compared by cosine similarity. Extraction uses GPT-4o constrained to strict JSON. A custom agent orchestration layer keeps state across a conversation.

PCA of Tunisian temperatures

Bachelor's thesis · extended
2024 · supervised by Prof. Chaouki Aouiti · team of 3

Given only twelve monthly averages per station and no geography at all, what structure does PCA recover from Tunisian climate data?

Two components explain 98.4% of the variation across 16 stations. The first is mean annual temperature (r = 0.9996). The second is the seasonal swing (r = 0.9893), and it separates coastal stations from inland ones using a label the model never received. PCA found the Mediterranean without being told where the sea is.

Image captioning with visual attention

Deep learning
2026 · reimplementation and comparison on Flickr8k

A faithful reimplementation of Show, Attend and Tell (Xu et al., 2015) against a Transformer decoder, plus a visual question answering model.

The reimplementation reaches BLEU-4 of 0.2224, slightly above the 0.2130 reported in the original paper. The Transformer decoder scored lower (0.2017), expected on 8,000 images, and worth reporting rather than hiding.

Attention entropy analysis: attention over nouns (3.7268) is measurably more concentrated than over function words (3.7883), against a uniform ceiling of 3.8918. The mechanism behaves as the paper claims, and the margin is smaller than the usual cherry-picked visualisations suggest.
